# Methods and interpretation

We use 2008 historical clickstream records from the UCI repository, not fabricated data. The unit of analysis is a recorded click. The UCI file is semicolon-delimited and has 14 original columns. The app labels the six screen-location codes 1–6 as top-left, top-middle, top-right, bottom-left, bottom-middle and bottom-right.

Counts by position use **clicked items** as the denominator. They are not click-through rates since the total number of viewed products or impressions is unavailable. Session IDs are anonymous and are not guaranteed to correspond to unique people. Maya is a fictional narrative character associated with anonymous session 18817, not an identifiable shopper.

This observational, click-only dataset cannot show causal effects of placement, why something was selected, unclicked items, total exposure, purchases or conversions. This older dataset from one store should not be represented as evidence of contemporary industry-wide shopping behaviour.

Filters are descriptive, not predictive or causal. Next steps could include an instrumented usability study with impressions and A/B testing, clearly separate from this dataset.
